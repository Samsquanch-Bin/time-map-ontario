Fort George stood on the west bank of the Niagara River near its mouth, facing the American Fort Niagara. In late May 1813 the Americans bombarded it from 25 May, and at dawn on 27 May they launched an amphibious assault on the lake shore west of the river mouth, supported by armed schooners. The first wave was led by Colonel Winfield Scott, with naval support directed by Oliver Hazard Perry. Brigadier General John Vincent's defenders, about 1,000 regulars plus militia including Captain Runchey's Company of Coloured Men, counter-attacked but suffered heavily from naval gunfire. Outnumbered and at risk of encirclement, Vincent ordered a retreat, and the Americans took the fort largely intact. Casualty figures vary between sources. Most of the British force escaped, falling back to Burlington Heights, and the Americans were slow to follow, which led to their check at Stoney Creek. The battlefield was designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1921.
