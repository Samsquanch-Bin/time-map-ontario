On 7 December 1837, during the Upper Canada Rebellion, government forces crushed an armed rising near John Montgomery's tavern on Yonge Street at Eglinton, north of Toronto. Reformers had grown frustrated after Lieutenant Governor Sir Francis Bond Head backed Tory candidates in the 1836 election, and William Lyon Mackenzie organized rebel militias north of the city. Under James FitzGibbon, about 1,200 government men with two cannons marched up Yonge Street. Rebels hidden in woods and behind stump fences opened fire, but artillery and a flanking detachment scattered them, and a cannonball through the tavern's dining room window sent the rest fleeing. Bond Head ordered the tavern burned. Many rebels escaped to the United States; Samuel Lount was later hanged for treason and others were transported to Van Diemen's Land. The site, now occupied by the 1936 Postal Station K building, was designated a National Historic Site in 1925.
