The Magasin Royal, or 'royal store', commonly known as Fort Douville, was a French fur-trading post built in 1720 beside the Humber River near today's Baby Point in Toronto. Philippe Dagneau dit Douville built it, by order of the Governor General of New France, the Marquis de Vaudreuil. One author described it as little more than a log cabin, and archaeologists have considered it the first non-Indigenous building in the Toronto area. The post lost its trade once the British finished Fort Oswego in 1727, and the French left it by the end of the 1720s. Because no other records exist, there was probably no permanent settlement in the Toronto area from 1730 to 1750, when the French returned to build Fort Toronto. Little is recorded about the building itself or whether any of it survives.
