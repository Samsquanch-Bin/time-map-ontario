Fort Toronto, also called Fort Portneuf, was a small French trading post built in 1750 near the mouth of the Humber River in present-day Toronto. The French officer Pierre Robineau de Portneuf built it to trade with First Nations in the Pays d'en Haut region of New France. It was the second French post in the Humber area, following the Magasin Royal of the 1720s. Fort Toronto drew traders right away, and its success led the French to build the larger Fort Rouillé nearby the following year. The French abandoned both posts during the Seven Years' War. After Britain captured New France, the trader Jean-Bonaventure Rousseau renovated the post and used it for the fur trade. The text does not say whether anything of it survives today.
