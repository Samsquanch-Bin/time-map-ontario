On 2 June 1866 Irish-American Fenians fought Canadian militia on Limestone Ridge near the village of Ridgeway, the largest engagement of the Fenian Raids. The Fenian Brotherhood hoped that seizing Canadian territory would pressure Britain over Irish independence. Under John O'Neill, a former Union officer, Fenians crossed the Niagara River on 1 June and the next morning took up a position on the ridge. About 850 Canadian militia, including the Queen's Own Rifles of Toronto, Hamilton's 13th Battalion and companies from Caledonia and York, advanced under Lieutenant Colonel Alfred Booker. After early success, confusion broke out in the Canadian ranks; accounts differ on why. Booker ordered a withdrawal and a Fenian bayonet charge routed the inexperienced militia. The Fenians briefly held Ridgeway, then withdrew toward Fort Erie and soon recrossed to the United States. It was the first battle fought only by Canadian troops under Canadian officers, and the battlefield is a National Historic Site.
