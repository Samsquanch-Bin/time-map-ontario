On 24 June 1813 an American column under Colonel Charles Boerstler, sent from Fort George, tried to surprise the British outpost at DeCou's stone house near Beaver Dams, in present-day Thorold. Laura Secord of Queenston had overheard American officers discussing the plan and walked through the woods to warn Lieutenant James FitzGibbon, confirming what Indigenous scouts had already reported. In thick woods east of Beaver Dams, about 300 Kahnawake warriors under Captain Dominique Ducharme and around 100 Mohawks under Captain William Johnson Kerr ambushed the column and wounded Boerstler. FitzGibbon, holding a small detachment of the 49th Foot in reserve, then approached under a flag of truce, claimed the Americans were surrounded, and persuaded them to give up. About 500 Americans became prisoners. FitzGibbon later wrote that only the Indigenous warriors had fired on his side. Afterwards the American garrison at Fort George seldom patrolled far from the fort. The site was recommended as a National Historic Site in 1921.
