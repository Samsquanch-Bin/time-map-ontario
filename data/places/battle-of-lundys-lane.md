Fought on 25 July 1814 near present-day Niagara Falls, Lundy's Lane was one of the bloodiest battles of the War of 1812. An invading American army under Major General Jacob Brown met a British and Canadian force under Lieutenant General Gordon Drummond, whose artillery stood in a hilltop cemetery along the lane. The fighting began around 6 p.m. and lasted until about midnight, with close-range musketry, bayonet fighting over the British guns, and friendly fire in the smoke and darkness. The Americans captured the guns, and Drummond made three counter-attacks to retake them. Brown, Winfield Scott and Drummond were all wounded, and Major General Phineas Riall was captured. Neither side clearly held the field, but the Americans withdrew to Fort Erie, leaving the British with the strategic initiative. Casualty figures vary by source. Part of the battlefield is preserved beside Drummond Hill Cemetery, where Laura Secord is buried, and the site became a National Historic Site in 1937.
