Fort George was built by the British Army at Niagara-on-the-Lake in 1796, when the Jay Treaty required them to give up Fort Niagara across the river. Planned largely as a supply depot, it grew into a ring of six earth-and-log bastions enclosing blockhouses, a hospital and other buildings. In 1812 it was headquarters of the British Army's central division, and Isaac Brock was buried in its northeast bastion after his death at Queenston Heights. American bombardment and the Battle of Fort George in May 1813 destroyed most of its buildings, and US forces held the fort until December 1813. Afterward it was neglected, replaced by Fort Mississauga, and used as farmland and a golf course. The Niagara Parks Commission rebuilt it in the late 1930s; the stone gunpowder magazine is the only original building left.
