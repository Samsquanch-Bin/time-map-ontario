Scadding Cabin is a log cabin built in 1794 for John Scadding, an immigrant from Devonshire, to meet the settlement duties attached to his land grant from the Crown. It first stood east of the Don River, south of Queen Street East, on a 253-acre lot that ran from Lake Ontario north to today's Danforth Avenue. Scadding lived in it until he went back to England in 1796. After his return to York in 1818 he sold the land and cabin to a farmer, William Smith, whose family used it as an outbuilding. In 1879 the Smiths gave the cabin to the York Pioneers, who took it apart and rebuilt it on the grounds of the first Industrial Exhibition, near the Fort Rouillé site. It is the oldest surviving building in Toronto and is now a museum furnished as an 1830s pioneer home.
