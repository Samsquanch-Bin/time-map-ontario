Teiaiagon, or Taiaiako'n, was a Haudenosaunee village on the east bank of the Humber River at today's Baby Point in Toronto, on the Toronto Carrying-Place Trail; its name means "it crosses the stream". Sources describe it as Seneca around 1685–1687, or as shared by the Seneca and Mohawk, and its full period of occupation is not precisely known. Étienne Brûlé is said to have passed through in 1615. The village lay among planted fields on a fur trade route, and La Salle's crew stopped here in 1678, when residents gave them food and traded corn. One estimate suggests 20 to 30 structures and 500 to 800 people, though another historian calls such figures speculation. The Seneca had left by 1701; accounts differ on whether Mississauga attack, a French campaign in 1687 or a voluntary move ended the village. Early digs found traces of palisade walls. Baby Point became a residential subdivision from 1912, and a plaque was placed there in 1949.
