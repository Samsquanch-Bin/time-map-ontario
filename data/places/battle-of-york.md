On 27 April 1813 an American force carried by Commodore Isaac Chauncey's squadron landed west of York, the capital of Upper Canada, now Toronto. Brigadier General Zebulon Pike led the landing. Mississauga and Ojibwe warriors under James Givins were first to oppose it, followed by British regulars and militia under Major General Roger Hale Sheaffe, who were outnumbered and pushed back. Judging the battle lost, Sheaffe withdrew his regulars toward Kingston, leaving the militia to seek terms, and had the fort's magazine blown up and a ship under construction burned. The explosion killed Pike and many of his soldiers. The Americans captured the fort, town and dockyard, and over the following days troops looted homes and burned the legislative buildings and Government House. They left on 8 May. Strategically the victory was limited, but stores seized for the Lake Erie squadron hurt the British there, and the burning of York was later cited as justification for the burning of Washington.
