The Mantle Site, renamed the Jean-Baptiste Lainé Site in 2011 to honour a Huron-Wendat Second World War veteran, is the largest and most complex ancestral Wendat village excavated so far in the Lower Great Lakes. Once dated to about 1500–1530, it has been re-dated by radiocarbon and Bayesian analysis to about 1587–1623, though some researchers dispute this. Some 1,500 to 2,000 people, likely drawn from several smaller villages including the Draper Site, lived on 4.2 hectares beside Stouffville Creek. A triple palisade enclosed 95 longhouses, at least 50 in use at once, laid out around an open plaza with organised refuse disposal. Finds include a European iron axehead, thought to have been traded inland from a Basque whaling station. Discovered during subdivision construction in 2002, the site was excavated from 2003 to 2005; about 5% was preserved and most of the rest is now a storm water pond and housing. Ontario Heritage Trust plaques in English, French and Wendat stand near Wendat Village Public School.
