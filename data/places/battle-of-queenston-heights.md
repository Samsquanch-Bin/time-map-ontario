The first major battle of the War of 1812 was fought at Queenston on 13 October 1812. Before dawn, American regulars and New York militia under Major General Stephen Van Rensselaer crossed the Niagara River from Lewiston. A party climbed the heights and seized a British gun position. Major General Isaac Brock, leading a counter-attack up the slope, was shot and killed, and a second attempt under Lieutenant Colonel John Macdonell also failed. Mohawk warriors from the Grand River under John Norton then harassed the Americans on the heights, unsettling many troops. On the American shore, militia refused to cross as reinforcements. In the afternoon Major General Roger Hale Sheaffe led British regulars, Canadian militia, Runchey's Coloured Company and Norton's Mohawks in an attack that forced the Americans to surrender; several hundred were captured, including Winfield Scott. The British victory came at the cost of Brock, who is buried with Macdonell beneath Brock's Monument on the heights.
