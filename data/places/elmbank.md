Elmbank was a rural hamlet at what is now Torbram Road and Britannia Road in Mississauga. Farmland in the area was cleared from the mid-1820s, and its first resident, John Grubb, came from Scotland in 1831, built a house above Etobicoke Creek and named the place Elmbank. The community grew to include a blacksmith, store, inn, schoolhouse, cheese factory, carriage maker and a Sons of Temperance hall, and had a post office from 1873 to 1915. A Catholic log church built in 1833, later replaced in brick, drew worshippers from Toronto. The population reached 300 in 1886 but had fallen to about 30 by 1926. After Malton was chosen for Toronto's airport in 1937, the land was expropriated and the buildings demolished. The last remnant, the Catholic cemetery, was moved in 2001. Nothing of the village remains.
