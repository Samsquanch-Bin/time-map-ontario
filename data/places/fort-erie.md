Old Fort Erie was the first fort Britain built in its new network after France ceded New France in 1763. The original 1764 post stood at the edge of the Niagara River and served as a supply depot and harbour for vessels carrying goods, troops and passengers across Lake Erie to the upper lakes. Storm damage led to plans in 1803 for a larger limestone fort on the higher ground behind it, still unfinished when war broke out in 1812. During the War of 1812 the fort changed hands several times. American troops captured it in July 1814 and were then besieged there; a British assault on 15 August 1814 was repulsed with about 1,000 casualties. The Americans destroyed the fort in November 1814. The ruins later passed to the Niagara Parks Commission, which reconstructed the fort in its 1812–1814 form, reopening it in 1939.
