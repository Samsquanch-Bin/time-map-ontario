Fort Rouillé was a French trading post completed in spring 1751 on what is now Exhibition Place in Toronto. Encouraged by the success of the small Fort Toronto built the year before, the Governor of New France ordered a larger post to intercept Indigenous traders travelling the Toronto Carrying-Place Trail toward the British post at Oswego. It was named after Antoine Louis Rouillé, the French minister of marine. The fort was a palisaded square of about 55 metres, with four corner bastions and five buildings. During the Seven Years' War its garrison was told to burn it if Fort Niagara fell; after that happened in July 1759, they set it on fire and withdrew to Montreal. Its remains were levelled in the 19th century. An obelisk raised in 1887 marks the site today, and the fort's outline is set in concrete on the ground.
