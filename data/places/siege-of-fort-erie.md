After Lundy's Lane, the American army withdrew to Fort Erie, which it had captured on 3 July 1814, and greatly extended its earthworks to Snake Hill and the lakeshore. Lieutenant General Gordon Drummond's British force arrived on 4 August and began a siege. On the night of 15–16 August Drummond sent three columns against the defences. Two were driven back with heavy losses, and a third broke into the fort's northeast bastion before a powder magazine beneath it exploded, killing many attackers. Through August and September Drummond's troops suffered from rain, sickness and poor shelter. On 17 September an American sortie under Brigadier General Peter B. Porter and Brigadier General James Miller captured two siege batteries and destroyed guns before being driven back, with heavy losses on both sides. Drummond lifted the siege on 21 September. The Americans later abandoned and demolished the fort on 5 November and went into winter quarters across the river.
