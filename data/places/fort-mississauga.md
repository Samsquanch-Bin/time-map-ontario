Fort Mississauga stands at Mississauga Point, where the Niagara River meets Lake Ontario. Neutral, Seneca and Mississauga peoples used the site before the fort, and in 1804 the first lighthouse on the Great Lakes was built there. During the War of 1812, the British set out to replace nearby Fort George. Beginning in spring 1814, Captain Runchey's Company of Coloured Men, working with the Royal Engineers, built the new fort using brick and stone salvaged from the ruins of Newark, which American forces had burned in December 1813, along with stone from the dismantled lighthouse. The fort was not completed until after the war. British troops were stationed there until 1855, and the Canadian militia later used it for training. The brick tower and star-shaped earthworks survive today within a golf course, as part of a National Historic Site.
