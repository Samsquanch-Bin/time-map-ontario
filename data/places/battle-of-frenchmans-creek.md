In the early hours of 28 November 1812, American troops crossed the Niagara River in a raid meant to prepare for a larger invasion planned by Brigadier General Alexander Smyth. One party under Captain William King, including navy sailors, stormed the British batteries at the Red House near Fort Erie and spiked the guns. A second party under Lieutenant Colonel Charles Boerstler landed near Frenchman's Creek, drove off a small detachment of the 49th Foot and fought Norfolk Militia, but managed to tear up only part of the bridge before withdrawing. British regulars, Lincoln Militia and allied Indigenous warriors then gathered under Lieutenant Colonel Cecil Bisshopp; King and his stranded men surrendered, and a relief force under Colonel William Winder was driven off under fire. Smyth's main invasion was postponed twice amid confusion and then abandoned, making the raid's success useless. Canadians named the fight after this creek; Americans called it the Affair opposite Black Rock. It is a National Historic Site of Canada.
