On 5 July 1814 American and British regulars met on open ground south of Chippawa Creek during the American invasion of Upper Canada along the Niagara River. Major General Jacob Brown's army had captured Fort Erie two days earlier and advanced north. Major General Phineas Riall, believing he faced a weak force, crossed the creek to attack. Early skirmishing in the woods involved British light infantry, militia and Indigenous warriors on one side and Brigadier General Peter B. Porter's volunteers and Iroquois allies on the other. The main clash came when Brigadier General Winfield Scott's well-drilled brigade, many in grey jackets, held firm under fire and caught the advancing British lines in a crossfire. After about 25 minutes Riall withdrew behind the creek. Casualty figures differ between official returns and later research, but British losses were heavy. The battle showed that trained American troops could match British regulars. The battlefield is now a National Historic Site of Canada.
