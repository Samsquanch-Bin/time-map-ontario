Bead Hill, on the Rouge River in what is now Rouge National Urban Park in Toronto, is the only known intact seventeenth-century Seneca site in Canada. Excavations in the 1980s suggested it could be Ganatsekwyagon, one of seven villages the Haudenosaunee set up along the north shore of Lake Ontario in the 1660s; the name likely means "among the birches". Test excavations suggest 500 to 800 people lived here for about 22 years, from about 1665 to 1687. The village was mainly a fur trading place at the meeting of the lakeshore trail and the Toronto Carrying-Place, and nineteenth-century accounts mention traces of a log palisade. The French priest Fénelon wintered here in 1669, the first recorded European stay near Toronto. After the French campaign against the Seneca in 1687 the village was abandoned; the source says it is unclear whether it was destroyed or its people withdrew south of the lake. A National Historic Site since 1991, it is not open to the public.
