Todmorden Mills was a small settlement in Toronto's Don River valley. In 1795 Lieutenant Governor John Graves Simcoe granted land on the Don to Aaron and Isaiah Skinner to build a mill that would supply lumber to the growing town of York, and their brother-in-law Parshall Terry joined them as a partner. A brewery was added beside the mill in 1820, run by Thomas Helliwell and John Eastwood. The name Todmorden came from Eastwood, whose family had emigrated from Todmorden in England. About 1855 the Taylor family bought both businesses and converted the mill to produce felt paper; it ran until the 1920s. The site later held riding stables and, in the early 1940s, a small camp for German prisoners of war. Opened as a museum in 1967, it keeps four original buildings, including the paper mill dating from 1825, the brewery and two houses.
