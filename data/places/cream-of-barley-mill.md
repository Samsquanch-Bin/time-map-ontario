The Cream of Barley Mill stands on Soper Creek on the outskirts of Bowmanville, in Clarington. Leonard Soper first built it as a grist mill in 1805, and several owners ran it after him. In 1884 the Scottish immigrant John MacKay bought the mill, then called the Caledonia Mill, to make a hot cereal he had invented, Cream of Barley, and installed new equipment of his own design for milling barley. The cereal sold widely across Canada and the British Empire. Fire destroyed the mill in 1904, and a new brick mill took its place. A campground, petting zoo and tourist park grew up beside it, run by the former champion runner Alfred Shrubb. Cold cereals ate into sales in the 1950s and the mill was sold. Since 1973 the protected heritage building has housed the Visual Arts Centre of Clarington.
