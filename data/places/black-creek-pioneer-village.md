The Village at Black Creek, formerly Black Creek Pioneer Village and before that Dalziel Pioneer Park, is an open-air heritage museum in Toronto's North York district, overlooking Black Creek, a tributary of the Humber River. It opened in 1960 and is run by the Toronto and Region Conservation Authority. The museum recreates rural Ontario life in the early-to-mid 19th century using more than forty buildings furnished in the style of the 1860s. A group of buildings put up by the Stong family still stands on its original sites. Most of the others were moved from elsewhere in Southern Ontario, including an 1850s blacksmith shop from Nobleton, a cooperage from Paris, an 1850 Temperance Hall from Kettleby, and the Roblin Mill from Ameliasburgh. Little is recorded in the source about the Stong family's own settlement of the land.
