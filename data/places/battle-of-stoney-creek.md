After capturing Fort George, an American force of about 3,400 under Brigadier Generals John Chandler and William Winder advanced west and camped at Stoney Creek on 5 June 1813. The British commander, Brigadier General John Vincent, had retreated to Burlington Heights. On reconnaissance, Lieutenant Colonel John Harvey judged the American camp poorly guarded and proposed a night attack. In the early hours of 6 June, about 700 British troops of the 8th and 49th Regiments struck the camp, with a small band of First Nations warriors under John Norton also present. Local tradition credits 19-year-old Billy Green with guiding the attackers, though how the password was obtained is disputed. The British lost surprise by cheering and suffered heavily, but a charge captured American guns, and both American generals were taken prisoner in the darkness. The leaderless Americans retreated, later falling back to Fort George. The battle became a turning point in the defence of Upper Canada; Battlefield House and a monument mark the site.
