The Ratcliff, or Baker Hill, Site is an ancestral Wendat (Huron) village in Whitchurch–Stouffville, about 25 kilometres north of Toronto, on a headwater stream of the Rouge River south of the Oak Ridges Moraine. The village covered roughly 2.8 hectares on the brow of a hill above a steep ravine. European trade goods such as black glass and copper beads suggest it was lived in between the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries. Its people probably came from the Mantle Site, five kilometres south-east, and lived here at the same time as the Aurora Site community. Items collected from the site included stone tools, pottery, pipes and bone needles; the pottery points to contact with Iroquoian peoples in present-day New York and the St. Lawrence Valley, and the quantity of stone work suggests the village made and traded stone tools. In the early 1950s the ravine was filled for a quarry, which still occupies the site, surrounded by farms.
