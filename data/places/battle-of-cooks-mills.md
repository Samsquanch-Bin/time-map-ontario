Cook's Mills on Lyon's Creek, in Crowland township, saw the last clash between American and British armies on the Niagara frontier in the War of 1812. After the siege of Fort Erie ended, General Gordon Drummond held a strong position behind Chippawa Creek. The American commander, Major General George Izard, sent about 1,200 men under Brigadier General Daniel Bissell to seize the mills and cut off a source of British flour. Drummond sent roughly 750 men under Colonel Christopher Myers to scout them. On the morning of 19 October the two forces fought for about half an hour; the Americans kept to the woods, and Myers, fearing he was being outflanked, retreated in good order. The Americans then destroyed the grain in the mill before withdrawing. Izard soon pulled back to Fort Erie, destroyed it and returned across the river, ending the fighting along the Niagara. The site was designated a National Historic Site in 1921.
