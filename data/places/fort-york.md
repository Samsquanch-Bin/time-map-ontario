Fort York began as a garrison set up by Lieutenant Governor John Graves Simcoe in 1793, when 100 Queen's Rangers landed near Garrison Creek and built log cabins to guard the harbour of the new settlement of York. Simcoe went ahead despite objections from the governor general, paying for the work from provincial rather than military funds. The post became an official British Army station in 1798. During the Battle of York in April 1813, the retreating British blew up the fort's gunpowder magazine, causing more than 250 American casualties, including General Zebulon Pike; the Americans later destroyed most of the fort. It was rebuilt from 1813 to about 1815 and served as a military hospital for the rest of the war. The military used the fort until the Second World War. It was restored to its early-19th-century form in 1934 and today operates as a museum.
