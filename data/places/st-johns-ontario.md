St. Johns, also known as Short Hills or Steel's Mills, is a rural hamlet in Thorold. It began in 1792, when Benjamin Canby built a sawmill on Twelve Mile Creek, whose flow stayed steady even in dry summers. More mills followed, and the settlement became the most important industrial centre on the Niagara Peninsula. A log cabin built by settler John Darling in 1799 became a school in 1804, said to be the first non-denominational free school in Upper Canada. By the 1830s the village had churches, a tannery, a woollen factory, a foundry, a tavern and a post office. During the Short Hills raid, rebels attacked British lancers at Osterhout's tavern. The Welland Canal and later the railways drew business away, and by 1900 little remained. The post office closed in 1914. The cabin school survives and is still used for teaching.
